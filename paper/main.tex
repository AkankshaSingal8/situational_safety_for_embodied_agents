\begin{abstract}
When a frozen vision-language-action (VLA) flow policy must avoid an obstacle its demonstrations never contained, the dominant recipe is to \emph{repair} its actions: project the generated chunk (or its intermediate denoising states) onto a control-barrier-safe set. We show this repair carries a large hidden tax: on obstacle-near-path cells of SafeLIBERO, an in-denoising CBF projection cuts paired task success from 67.5\% to 32.5\% relative to noise-space alternatives (exact McNemar $p=0.0066$), while buying no additional safety. Our thesis is that enforcement belongs in the policy's \emph{noise space}, not its action space: because the frozen $10$-step flow map is a deterministic function of the initial noise, safety can be obtained by \emph{selecting}, \emph{tilting} (Feynman--Kac reweighting), or \emph{ascending} (adjoint gradients through the denoise map) the noise --- every emitted action stays exactly on the policy manifold --- optionally composed with a soft repulsor field on the velocity target. These operators trace a smooth, tunable success--safety Pareto frontier (71.2/17.5 $\to$ 60.0/50.0 $\to$ 48.8/57.5 TSR/CAR on Spatial~L1), certified by a single mechanism-independent \emph{checked prefix certificate}: a discrete-CBF chain re-verified on the executed actions, sound regardless of which operator produced them. Identification of \emph{what} to avoid is ground-truth-free at zero measured cost: a three-literal first-order rule over VLM-groundable predicates is statistically indistinguishable from ground-truth identity in paired rollouts (all $p\ge0.18$), and swapping hand-coded predicate heuristics for VLM predicate grounding changes no decision. On LIBERO-Safety, training-free noise-space steering of the \emph{off-the-shelf} $\pi_{0.5}$ policy --- never safety-trained --- eliminates violations (30\% and 48\% $\to$ 0\%) and matches or beats the benchmark's dedicated 19.6k-demonstration safety fine-tune on Safe-Success, while sweeping the safety-reasoning refusal track (100/100/80 vs.\ best published 80/72/36).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Flow-matching VLA policies such as $\pi_{0.5}$ decode an action chunk by integrating a learned velocity field from Gaussian noise. They imitate expert demonstrations superbly and unsafely: on SafeLIBERO --- LIBERO tasks with a hazardous distractor placed near the reach path --- the frozen policy collides in up to 86--88\% of episodes, and on LIBERO-Safety the off-the-shelf checkpoint drives its gripper into a human hand in 40\% of episodes on the hardest suite. The prevailing fix is \emph{repair}: intercept the policy's action (after generation, or at intermediate denoising steps) and project it onto a control-barrier-function (CBF) safe set. Repair is attractive because it is training-free and certificate-friendly. This paper argues, with paired evidence, that it is also the wrong intervention point.

\textbf{Pillar 1: the repair tax.} We run five enforcement mechanisms head-to-head on identical seeds, identical hazard identity, and an identical safety certificate, so that the enforcement operator is the \emph{only} difference (Sec.~\ref{sec:pilots}). On obstacle-near-path dev cells, in-denoising CBF projection --- our own strongest tuned variant of the state-of-the-art recipe \cite{sota2607} --- achieves 32.5\% task success. Merely \emph{deleting the repair} and keeping best-of-$K$ noise-seed selection lifts success to 55.0\%; first-order noise-space search reaches 67.5\% (paired exact McNemar $p=0.0066$) with collisions statistically unchanged. The projection's off-manifold corrections are the success killer: they produce action sequences the policy never learned to continue.

\textbf{Pillar 2: a certified, tunable frontier in noise space.} The frozen denoise map $F:(z,\mathrm{obs})\mapsto\text{chunk}$ is deterministic, so the safe behaviors of the policy form a \emph{preimage} $S=\{z: F(z)\ \text{passes the safety check}\}$. We instantiate three noise-space operators --- \textsc{Select} (zeroth-order best-of-$K$), \textsc{Tilt} (Feynman--Kac particle reweighting at mid-denoising steps, essentially free), and \textsc{Ascend} (reverse-mode gradients of the clearance margin through all 10 Euler steps, 57\,ms per gradient step) --- plus a soft annealed repulsor field added to the velocity target. Success and safety turn out to be \emph{separable}: FK tilting carries task success (71.2/17.5 TSR/CAR on full Spatial L1, beating the unguided baseline on both axes), the repulsor carries safety (60.0/50.0) and is the only mechanism that un-sticks obstacle-on-grasp geometry (50\% success where every hard projection scores 0--10\%), and their composition spans a smooth Pareto frontier (Sec.~\ref{sec:pilots}). All operating points inherit one certificate: a discrete-CBF chain \emph{re-checked on the executed action prefix}, whose soundness is a property of the output and therefore independent of the search mechanism (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}).

\textbf{Pillar 3: identification is GT-free at zero cost.} Deciding \emph{what} to avoid does not require privileged simulator state, and --- more surprisingly --- does not require a VLM to make the decision. A three-literal first-order rule,
$\textsc{Hazard}(x) \coloneqq \textsc{Protected}(x)\vee\textsc{Moving}(x)\vee(\lnot\textsc{Mentioned}(x)\wedge\textsc{NearestToPath}(x))$,
matches ground-truth identity in paired rollouts (no significant difference on any axis, all $p\ge0.18$; every trend favors the rule, Sec.~\ref{sec:e3}). The VLM's proper role is \emph{grounding predicates}, not making scene-level decisions: replacing the rule's hand heuristics with VLM-answered per-object predicates (\textsc{Referenced}, \textsc{Protected}) reproduces the identical decision on every scene of two benchmarks, while adding paraphrase competence no heuristic has (``deliver it to me'' $\Rightarrow$ the hand is protected). Both disproven alternatives --- whole-scene VLM identification and runtime VLM rule \emph{composition} --- fail unsafe (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).

The three pillars assemble into a training-free system for frozen flow VLAs, evaluated at $n{=}200$ per cell on SafeLIBERO against a five-row architecture board (baseline, post-hoc shield, reimplemented SOTA, ours with and without ground truth), and ported to LIBERO-Safety where the centerpiece result is architectural: \emph{steering an off-the-shelf policy matches a dedicated safety fine-tune} (Sec.~\ref{sec:ls}). We report an unusually complete negative-results section (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}); in our experience the failed mechanisms delineate the contribution as sharply as the successful ones.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organize prior safety interventions for diffusion/flow VLAs by \emph{where they touch the generation pipeline}; the map also locates our contribution.

\textbf{Prompt/conditioning.} $\pi_{0.7}$-style multimodal prompt conditioning steers task behavior through language and visual subgoals; we find experimentally (four testbeds, Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}) that safety instructions in the prompt move task success but never collision avoidance --- the language channel is behaviorally live yet safety-inert.

\textbf{Activations.} An activation-steering line edits VLA hidden states for task/concept control (COAST \cite{coast}; causal speed/direction directions; PID-style closed-loop variants), and ``Your Model Already Knows'' \cite{yourmodel} \emph{reads} attention heads for target/obstacle identification feeding a CBF. None writes activations for safety with a certificate; the read-only identification route is a direct competitor to our identification chapter.

\textbf{Velocity field.} In-denoising guidance modifies the flow's velocity target or intermediate chunk: the direct predecessor \cite{sota2607} imposes a discrete exponential CBF along the horizon inside $\pi_{0.5}$'s denoising; OmniGuide \cite{omniguide} injects task guidance on $\pi_{0.5}$/GR00T; VLS \cite{vls} injects VLM reward gradients with FK resampling into frozen $\pi_{0.5}$ denoising for OOD generality. VLS is mechanically the closest work to our \textsc{Tilt}/\textsc{Ascend} operators, but targets performance, not safety, and carries no certificate. \cite{sota2607} uses the same textbook DCBF background we do \cite{agrawal2017dcbf} but proves nothing about its own pipeline: its min-norm correction ignores actuation limits, corrections at intermediate steps are never re-verified on the final chunk, and infeasibility is unhandled.

\textbf{Intermediate states.} Constricting Barrier Functions \cite{cbf2602} certify geometric constraints on intermediate diffusion states; SafeDiffuser-style projections \cite{safediffuser} are known to trap samples on constraint boundaries --- an in-generation form of the repair tax.

\textbf{Output/commit.} Post-hoc shields project the final action (AEGIS \cite{aegis}; classical CBF-QP \cite{ames2019cbf,brunke2025semantic}); PACS \cite{pacs} verifies chunk-plus-failsafe trajectories against reachable human occupancy on flow VLAs --- the strongest certified output-stage work, whose OOD critique of CBF push-away converges with our repair-tax measurement from the opposite side; commit-stage selectors choose among finished chunks.

\textbf{Noise space.} Noise-space manipulation of flow policies exists for \emph{performance} (initial-noise optimization, flow-reversal and DSRL-style latent-action RL); to our knowledge no prior work performs \emph{safety} enforcement in noise space, and none pairs any enforcement mechanism against matched-seed in-denoising CBF repair. That unoccupied lane --- noise-space operators under a checked, mechanism-independent certificate, with a paired quantification of the repair tax they avoid --- is this paper.

\textbf{Benchmarks and identification.} We evaluate on SafeLIBERO \cite{sota2607} and LIBERO-Safety \cite{liberosafety}; semantic-constraint compilation from VLMs follows Brunke et al.\ \cite{brunke2025semantic}, whose corridor-exemption idea (relax margins along sanctioned approaches) transfers to our setting while their superquadric shapes do not (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).

\section{Problem Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

An episode provides an instruction $\ell$, observations (RGB views, depth, proprioception), and a frozen flow VLA ($\pi_{0.5}$ \cite{pi05}) that decodes an $H$-step action chunk by $N{=}10$ Euler steps from noise $z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$; the first 5 steps of each chunk are executed before replanning. A designated hazard must not be disturbed while the task completes within the step budget. Metrics: task success rate (TSR) and collision avoidance rate (CAR) on SafeLIBERO (collision $=$ $>$1\,mm total ground-truth hazard displacement, evaluation-side only); on LIBERO-Safety, the benchmark's own contact-predicate violation metric and SafeSR ($=$ success with zero violations).

\emph{Privilege tiers.}\footnote{Tier definitions, stated exactly: \textbf{Tier-GT} reads hazard identity and all object positions from the simulator (the comparison tier of prior in-denoising work). \textbf{Tier-Percep (no-GT)} reads the instruction, images, depth, proprioception, and the symbolic object \emph{name} list only; hazard identity, positions, extents, priors, and corridor anchors are perception- or VLM-derived. Collision \emph{scoring} is always GT-anchored in both tiers. Pixel-only naming (dropping the name list) is measured and reported as future work (top-2 identification 19$\to$12 of 19).} All method claims are made at the tier stated per table; the headline no-GT rows use the fully general stack (VLM priors, not benchmark-tuned tables).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Identification: a first-order rule over VLM-grounded predicates}
\label{sec:ident}

The scene-level decision of what to guard is made by a fixed three-literal rule:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\textsc{Hazard}(x) \coloneqq{}& \textsc{Protected}(x)\ \vee\ \textsc{Moving}(x)\ \vee \\
&\big(\lnot\textsc{Mentioned}(x)\wedge\textsc{NearestToPath}(x)\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fol}
\end{equation}
ranked protected-first, then by path distance. \textsc{Protected} covers a semantic class (body parts, objects attached to a person) that is never mention-excluded and always floored onto the guard path; \textsc{Moving} is grounded online by settle-window displacement ($>$1\,cm), re-read at every replan --- this single class rule is what handles dynamic human-hand intruders. \textsc{Mentioned} uses content-token overlap with a head-noun rule; \textsc{NearestToPath} measures distance to the sanctioned reach segments (end-effector $\to$ target $\to$ destination).

\emph{Predicate grounding, two interchangeable backends.} The literals can be grounded by (a) hand heuristics plus one-shot cached VLM hazard-property priors with a fail-safe floor of $0.5$ (semantics may raise concern, never delete protection), or (b) \emph{VLM predicate grounding}: a small VLM (3 votes, cached per task, runtime API-free) answers only local per-object questions --- \textsc{Referenced}$(x)$ (reference resolution including paraphrase) and \textsc{Protected}$(x)$. On 59 LIBERO-Safety scenes the two backends produce the \emph{identical} decision on every scene (obstacle suite 12/13 top-1/top-2 both; human suite 12/12 both), while the VLM backend resolves cases no string heuristic can (``deliver it to me'' $\Rightarrow$ \texttt{left\_hand} protected; a plate held by a hand both \textsc{Referenced} and \textsc{Protected}). The division of labor is the design finding: \emph{the VLM answers local predicates; the logic makes the decision.} Both ways of giving the VLM more authority fail measurably (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).

Positions and extents at the no-GT tier come from open-vocabulary detection \cite{gdino} back-projected through depth (SafeLIBERO median error 0.067\,m; LIBERO-Safety 0.036--0.038\,m), with percentile-box extents; localization failures fall back conservatively and are logged per episode.

\subsection{Enforcement: noise-space operators over a frozen flow map}
\label{sec:operators}

Let $F(z,\mathrm{obs})$ be the frozen 10-step Euler map and let $M(a_{1:L})$ be the minimum DCBF chain margin of the executed $L$-step prefix under the barrier of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}. All operators search the noise preimage $S=\{z: M(F(z))\ge 0\}$; emitted actions are always $F(z^\ast)$, exactly on the policy manifold.

\textbf{\textsc{Select} (zeroth order).} Sample $K{=}8$ seeds sharing one vision-language KV cache; score decoded candidates lexicographically (chain feasibility $\succ$ clearance margin $\succ$ task progress); execute the winner's prefix.

\textbf{\textsc{Tilt} (Feynman--Kac).} Reweight and resample the $K$-particle population at two mid-denoising steps under soft margin potentials $\exp(\beta\,\tilde M)$; with potentials off the sampler is bit-identical to \textsc{Select}. Computationally near-free.

\textbf{\textsc{Ascend} (first order).} Warm-start from the \textsc{Select} winner and take 3--5 reverse-mode gradient steps of the prefix margin through all 10 Euler steps with respect to $z$ (prefix KV stop-gradiented; the 3B backbone is never touched). Measured: 57\,ms per gradient step, 6.9\,GB peak, noise remains $\approx\mathcal N(0,I)$ (std 0.954).

\textbf{Soft repulsor field.} An annealed analytic repulsive velocity added to the flow's velocity target during denoising (strength $\eta$). Not a noise-space operator, but the natural \emph{soft} complement: it shapes the vector field continuously instead of projecting states, and is the only mechanism we found that navigates obstacle-on-grasp geometry that every hard projection locks up.

\textbf{Baseline operator: in-denoising repair.} Our control arm is the composed CBF projection sweep (schedule, corridor exemption, companion points) --- a strictly stronger tuned variant of \cite{sota2607}, and the production configuration of the SafeLIBERO board (Table~\ref{tab:board}). The pilots of Sec.~\ref{sec:pilots} compare all operators against it under paired seeds.

\subsection{Certificate}
\label{sec:theory}

Actuation model: the end-effector is a discrete single integrator over the chunk, $p_j=p_{j-1}+s\,u_j$, $u_j\in[-c,c]^3$; tracking error is absorbed into the safety margin $d_{\mathrm{safe}}$ (assumption A1$'$), and obstacle motion is bounded by the per-step inflation $\lambda$ (A2; $\lambda=4$\,mm at 20\,Hz $\Rightarrow$ movers $\le$8\,cm/s). The barrier is
$B_j(p)=\min_{m,q}\lVert p+q-o_m\rVert-r_m(j)$
over obstacles $m$ and companion offsets $q$ (wrist $+8$\,cm, fingertip/payload $-6$\,cm), with $r_m(j)=r_{\mathrm{eff},m}+\lambda j$. Safety of a length-$L$ prefix is $B_j\ge0$ for $j\le L$ under the chain condition $B_j\ge(1-\gamma)B_{j-1}$, $\gamma=0.9$.

\begin{lemma}[Geometric decay; textbook \cite{agrawal2017dcbf}]
If $B_0>0$ and the chain holds for $j\le L$, then $B_j\ge(1-\gamma)^jB_0>0$: the certified prefix never enters the hazard set.
\end{lemma}

We claim no novelty for the induction; \cite{sota2607} uses it identically as background. Our contributions are the following three statements (proofs in the appendix, checked line-by-line against the implementation).

\begin{theorem}[Feasibility under actuation limits]\label{thm:feas}
For every state with $B_{j-1}\ge0$ there exists an admissible $u_j\in[-c,c]^3$ satisfying the chain condition, and the brake-then-push operator returns one: the brake ($u_j$ with all approach components removed) never decreases the barrier, and whenever $B_{j-1}\ge\lambda/\gamma$ ($4.5$\,mm deployed) the brake alone satisfies the chain; the saturated push strictly improves on it along the radial escape direction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Checked prefix certificate]\label{prop:cert}
The acceptance stage re-evaluates the chain on the executed prefix of the \emph{final decoded chunk} by explicit rollout of the actuation model. If the check passes, the prefix satisfies the chain \emph{regardless of how the chunk was produced} --- attenuated repairs, saturated pushes, or any noise-space operator. If all $K$ candidates fail, the argmax-margin candidate executes with its true margin reported (fail-visible, not fail-silent).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Selection soundness]\label{prop:select}
If at least one of $K$ candidates satisfies the prefix chain, the executed chunk satisfies it; lexicographic selection never prefers an infeasible candidate over a feasible one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Mechanism independence]\label{cor:mech}
\textsc{Select}, \textsc{Tilt}, and \textsc{Ascend} are zeroth-, importance-weighted-, and first-order search over the same safe preimage $S$; Prop.~\ref{prop:cert} certifies membership in $S$ by an output check, so the certificate is identical across all enforcement mechanisms in this paper. The paper's claims separate cleanly into (i) the certificate and (ii) the empirical ranking of search procedures.
\end{corollary}

Correction-based soundness arguments (``the repair enforces the chain'') cannot cover noise-space operators; the checked-output semantics can, and is what prior in-denoising work lacked even for its own repairs. Stated non-claims: rotation channels are uncertified (partially covered by companions); no formal perception-robustness radius is claimed --- the fail-direction study quantifies it empirically.

\subsection{Three-regime routing}
\label{sec:routing}

Not every hazard is a keep-out. The identification layer routes each episode into one of three regimes:
(1) \textbf{Avoid} --- a geometric hazard off the task path: noise-space enforcement as above.
(2) \textbf{Duality disengage} --- the hazard \emph{is} the destination (\textsc{Protected}$(x)\wedge$\textsc{Referenced}$(x)$, or the guard overlaps the parsed destination by $<$0.15\,m): geometric keep-out disengages for the episode; safety comes from routing, not barriers. Empirically forced by the human-handover suites, where the policy's trained behavior already avoids hand contact (zero violations unguided) and any keep-out only destroys the task (Sec.~\ref{sec:ls}).
(3) \textbf{Refuse} --- the \emph{instruction itself} is the hazard: a rule-based semantic judge (verb $\times$ patient $\times$ hazard-property triples) refuses execution (offline F1 0.968 on LIBERO-Safety instructions, precision 0.938 / recall 1.000; in-domain upper bound --- the lexicon was written against this suite's vocabulary).

\section{Experiments IV: LIBERO-Safety}
\label{sec:ls}

The identification stack transfers with zero benchmark tuning beyond the two class rules already in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fol}.\footnote{Provenance note (honest-baseline strategy): the released fine-tuned checkpoint reproduces the paper's protocol mechanics in our harness (94\% TSR on reasoning-safety through the same client) but scores 12--22 TSR on obstacle-avoidance where the paper reports 58--63, at the paper's own 600-step horizon. \section{Negative Results}
\label{sec:negative}

Each mechanism below was implemented, pre-registered, and killed by its own gate; we report them because they bound the design space.

\textbf{Renoise repair} (Sec.~\ref{sec:pilots}): no gain over rejection sampling at equal compute, $p=1.0$; NO-GO.

\textbf{Safety prompting is not a safety mechanism.} Across four testbeds --- SafeLIBERO pilots ($n{=}40$: prompt-only 75.0/12.5 vs.\ baseline 67.0/14.0 --- a TSR trend with CAR flat) and LIBERO-Safety L0 ($n{=}50$: obstacle prompt-baseline 20 TSR/8 violations vs.\ 12/4, violations \emph{up}; human 46/0 vs.\ 54/0; prompting also hurts the guided arms) --- the language channel moves task behavior, never avoidance. Negative-prompt guidance was accordingly retired before rollout.

\textbf{Scene graphs: NO-GO, three times.} Graph-structured identification exactly ties the flat scorer wherever hazards are objects (SafeLIBERO 19/19 agreement; LIBERO-Safety obstacle 9/11 twice), and collapses identically on the relational human suite (0/2 top-2) --- where the fix is a 10-line \textsc{Protected} class rule (12/12), not structure.

\textbf{VLM rule composition (FOL-compile).} With \emph{oracle} grounding the entire compositional apparatus gains at most one scene (via \textsc{Moving}, adoptable as a flat class rule) and loses one to selection brittleness. With real VLM critics (two vendors) composition is strictly worse than the flat rule on every suite --- haiku 13/16, gemini 11/13 vs.\ flat 16/19 on SafeLIBERO --- and the characteristic failure is over-restrictive conjunctions that fire on \emph{nothing}: the true hazard goes unguarded, fail-unsafe (2 and 10 empty-rule scenes respectively). Bounded on both sides, the direction is closed; its surviving artifacts are the two class rules and the predicate-grounding division of labor (Sec.~\ref{sec:ident}).

\textbf{Whole-scene VLM identification} scores 18--64\% (API-strength models) and 0--10\% (local models, direct prompting) where the decomposed rule scores 100\% on the same scenes --- the decomposition claim survives at every model scale we tested.

\textbf{Shape ablations.} Superquadric/AABB barriers, corridor-relaxed shapes, and a faithful relation-conditioned ``above'' envelope all trade TSR for CAR without dominating the thin sphere + corridor; the semantic ideas that \emph{did} transfer from the shape literature are the corridor exemption and the protected class, not the geometry.

\section{Limitations and Discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Honesty items.} All SafeLIBERO comparisons vs.\ the SOTA method are against our faithful self-run reimplementation under one criterion (their published CAR is not reproducible under our criterion at any radius; see footnote to Table~\ref{tab:board}); all LIBERO-Safety claims are against our self-run baseline on the released checkpoint (provenance footnote, Sec.~\ref{sec:ls}). LIBERO-Safety cells are $n{=}50$ single-seed ($\approx\pm11$pp CIs); we lean only on unambiguous cells and paired tests. The SSR judge lexicon is in-domain (15 unique unsafe instructions); the quoted F1 is an upper bound pending held-out phrasings. The no-GT tier consumes the symbolic object \emph{name} list; pixel-only naming currently costs identification (19$\to$12 of 19 top-2) and is future work.

\textbf{Structural limitations.} Obstacle-on-grasp cells resist every hard mechanism (the soft repulsor's 50\% is the current ceiling); Long-horizon liveness stalls afflict all barrier-class methods including ours; identity errors fail unsafe at $k{=}1$ (mitigated by the guard-set mode); rotation is uncertified; benchmark-definition divergence (Long L2) shows a semantically-correct hazard model can disagree with a benchmark's arbitrary designated-obstacle label.

\textbf{Real-robot discussion.} \todo{real-robot transfer discussion: sensing at real error floors (our margins are tuned to simulation-grade localization, median 3.6--6.7\,cm), OSC tracking-error measurement for A1$'$, latency budget of \textsc{Ascend}/\textsc{Tilt} at control rate, and the moving-intruder bound (A2, $\le$8\,cm/s) against natural human hand speeds.}

\section{Conclusion}
Enforcement for frozen flow VLAs belongs in noise space. Hard repair --- post-hoc or in-denoising --- pays a paired, significant success tax and is never the safety winner; selecting, tilting, or ascending the noise keeps every action on the policy manifold and, composed with a soft velocity field, spans a certified, tunable success--safety frontier under one checked, mechanism-independent certificate. What to avoid needs no privileged state and no VLM authority: a three-literal rule with VLM-grounded predicates matches ground truth at zero cost. And the sharpest system-level consequence is architectural: training-free steering of an off-the-shelf policy can match a dedicated safety fine-tune.

\section*{Appendix: Proof Sketches}
\textbf{Thm.~\ref{thm:feas}.} With the brake $u_j$ (approach components removed), $\lVert p_j+q-o_m\rVert\ge\lVert p_{j-1}+q-o_m\rVert$ for the argmin pair, so $B_j\ge B_{j-1}-\lambda$ under inflation growth; whenever $\gamma B_{j-1}\ge\lambda$ this implies the chain $B_j\ge(1-\gamma)B_{j-1}$. The push channel applies the correction along the radial escape direction of the argmin (obstacle, companion) pair, along which the effective distance strictly increases (for the sphere trivially; for the superquadric the shape term is constant along the center ray while distance grows); if the required push exceeds $[-c,c]^3$ the operator falls back to the full brake, which never decreases the barrier --- the executed step is never a known-violating step. Below the $\lambda/\gamma$ corner the push channel engages and Prop.~\ref{prop:cert} is what guarantees the executed prefix (checked, not assumed). \emph{Assumptions:} single-integrator kinematics with linear command scaling (A1); tracking error absorbed by $d_{\mathrm{safe}}=0.01$\,m (A1$'$, no controller proof attempted); obstacle displacement $\le\lambda$ per step within a chunk (A2); perception error shifts $o_m$ and the certificate is relative to the estimate; the chain covers the translational executed prefix only (5 steps deployed), suffixes are re-planned.

\textbf{Prop.~\ref{prop:cert}.} The acceptance stage rolls out $p_j=p_{j-1}+s\,u_j$ on the decoded chunk and evaluates the chain at full margins for $j\le L$; passing is a predicate of the emitted actions alone. Failure of all $K$ candidates triggers argmax-margin execution with the true margin reported.

\textbf{Prop.~\ref{prop:select}.} The lexicographic scalarization gives feasibility a dominating weight; ties among feasible candidates break by margin then progress. $\;\blacksquare$
